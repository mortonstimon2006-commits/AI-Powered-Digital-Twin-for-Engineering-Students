# Writing a clear, honest technical resume

## Structure

For students, a single page is usually enough. A common order is:

1. Contact details and links (GitHub, portfolio, LinkedIn).
2. Education (degree, institution, graduation year, CGPA if appropriate).
3. Skills, grouped by category.
4. Projects, with 2–4 bullet points each.
5. Experience (internships, research, part-time roles).
6. Certifications and achievements.

## Write strong bullet points

- Start with a specific action verb: built, designed, implemented, analysed, automated, optimised, reduced.
- Say what you did, how you did it, and what changed as a result.
- Quantify only when you have real numbers, for example "reduced query time from 4 s to 0.3 s by adding an index". **Never invent metrics.**
- Avoid vague phrases such as "worked on", "helped with" or "responsible for" without saying what you actually did.

## Make skills credible

Every skill in your skills section should appear in at least one project or experience bullet that shows how you used it. A long list of technologies with no supporting evidence looks weaker than a short list with clear examples.

## Tailor to the role

Compare your resume with the job description. Highlight the projects and skills that match, and use the role's terminology where it accurately describes your work. TwinPath's resume and job-description analyser shows which required skills your resume mentions and which are missing.

## Honesty matters

Only list skills, projects, certifications and experience you actually have. Interviewers ask follow-up questions about everything on the page, and misrepresentation can lead to offers being withdrawn.
