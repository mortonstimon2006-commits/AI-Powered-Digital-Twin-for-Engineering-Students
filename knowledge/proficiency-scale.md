# The TwinPath proficiency scale

TwinPath uses one 0–4 scale for self-ratings, evidence claims and role requirements, so they can be compared directly.

## Level 0 — None

No exposure yet. Example: you have never written a SQL query.

## Level 1 — Awareness

You can follow a tutorial and explain the basic terms, but you need step-by-step guidance. Example: you can write a simple `SELECT ... WHERE` query by copying a pattern from a tutorial.

## Level 2 — Working

You complete guided or familiar tasks on your own, but unfamiliar problems still need help. Example: you can write joins and aggregations to answer routine questions on a schema you know.

## Level 3 — Proficient

You build non-trivial solutions independently, and you debug and test them. Example: you design queries with window functions and CTEs for new analytical questions, check the results, and can explain an execution plan.

## Level 4 — Advanced

You design systems, make informed trade-offs and can mentor others. Example: you design schemas and indexing strategies for a production workload and review other people's queries for correctness and performance.

## How to rate yourself honestly

- Rate what you can do **today without looking up every step**, not what you studied once.
- If you are unsure between two levels, choose the lower one and then gather evidence (a project or an assessment) to move up.
- Self-ratings carry the least weight in TwinPath. Linking a project, or asking a mentor to record an assessment, gives your estimate far more credibility.

## Moving up a level

Moving up one level typically takes deliberate practice on progressively harder tasks, plus feedback. TwinPath's roadmap uses planning ranges of roughly 5–10 hours to reach level 1, 10–20 hours for level 2, 20–40 hours for level 3, and 40–80 hours for level 4. Real effort varies widely from person to person.
