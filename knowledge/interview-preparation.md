# Preparing for entry-level technical interviews

Hiring processes differ between organisations, and TwinPath has no access to any company's interview process. This guide describes common formats and preparation habits that are widely useful.

## Common interview stages

- **Online coding assessment**: timed problems on data structures and algorithms, usually auto-graded.
- **Technical interview**: live problem solving while explaining your reasoning, plus questions about your projects and fundamentals (databases, operating systems, networking, object-oriented design).
- **Role-specific round**: for example, SQL and case questions for data analysts, model evaluation questions for ML roles, or API and database design for backend roles.
- **Behavioural / HR round**: teamwork, conflict, motivation and communication.

## Preparing for coding rounds

1. Revise core data structures (arrays, hash maps, stacks, queues, trees, heaps, graphs) and their time complexities.
2. Practise problems by **pattern** (two pointers, sliding window, BFS/DFS, binary search, dynamic programming, greedy) rather than at random.
3. Do timed sessions. After each one, review the solution and note the pattern you missed.
4. Practise thinking aloud: state assumptions, discuss a brute-force approach, then optimise, then test with edge cases.

## Explaining your projects

Interviewers often spend a lot of time on your projects. For each project, prepare:

- the problem it solves and who uses it;
- your personal contribution (not just the team's);
- one technical decision you made and the alternatives you rejected;
- one bug or failure and how you diagnosed it;
- what you would improve with more time.

Only describe work you actually did. Inflated claims are easy to expose with a single follow-up question.

## Behavioural questions: the STAR structure

Structure stories as **Situation, Task, Action, Result**. Keep the focus on your own actions, and quantify the result where you honestly can. Prepare 5–6 stories that can be adapted to several questions: a challenge, a failure, a disagreement, leading something, learning something quickly.

## Mock interviews

Mock interviews with a mentor are one of the most effective preparation methods because they give feedback on communication as well as correctness. In TwinPath, a mentor can record the mock-interview result as a verified assessment, which strengthens your digital twin.

## On the day

Read the problem carefully, ask clarifying questions, and manage your time. If you get stuck, say what you are thinking. Interviewers usually assess your reasoning, not only the final answer.
